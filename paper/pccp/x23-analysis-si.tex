\section{Molecular-Crystal Energies and Numerical Controls}
\label{sec:x23_si}

\subsection{Geometries and electronic protocol}
\label{sec:crystal-protocol}

The crystal and gas-phase geometries are taken from Sec.~5.6 of the versioned supporting dataset arXiv:2402.13059v1 of Della Pia \emph{et al.}\cite{DellaPia2024X23} Crystal coordinates are wrapped into the supplied cells without relaxation or symmetrization. CO$_2$ and NH$_3$ have cubic cells with $a=5.624$ and 5.1305~\AA{} and four molecules each. Urea has a tetragonal cell with $a=b=5.565$, $c=4.684$~\AA{}, and two molecules. The separately supplied gas molecules are rigidly translated into centred cells whose dimensions are the molecular extents plus 25~\AA{}. They use nonperiodic cell/Poisson settings and the analytic isolated-system solver.

All four representations use Skala-1.1 Rev1 on a smoothly partitioned atom-composite quadrature. The baseline quadrature has 150 radial points and the 770-point Lebedev rule. Five multigrids use cutoffs of 800/60~Ry in both phases. The baseline AE calculations use the UZH 2026.2 TZVPP-MOLOPT-PBE-ae basis and explicit nuclei. All three reported AE pairs instead use QZVPP-MOLOPT-PBE-ae with 200 radial points and the 974-point Lebedev rule, following the matched tests in Sec.~\ref{sec:crystal-basis}. GTH calculations retain TZV2P-MOLOPT-PBE-GTH bases, GTH-PBE potentials, and the baseline quadrature. One-centre calculations employ the small extended expansion and accurate augmented-XC integration. The AE hard-minus-soft fields are reconstructed before the joint model evaluation. Direct and reconstructed GTH calculations share their Hamiltonian and basis.

D3(BJ) is included once, with $s_6=1$, $s_8=1.9889$, $a_1=0.3981$, $a_2=4.4211$, and interaction cutoff 20 in the executed input convention.\cite{Grimme2010DFTD3,Grimme2011D3BJ} The B3LYP parameter alias selects these damping constants, not the electronic functional. All systems are neutral restricted singlets. OT convergence is $5\times10^{-7}$. Complex k-point orbitals use iterative refinement of the approximative congruence transformation with the full AE preconditioner. No density renormalization, energy alignment, or fitting to the reference lattice energies is applied.

Every crystal uses a Gamma-centred $3^3$ mesh with full point-group reduction. With symmetry tolerance $10^{-6}$, baseline AE and GAPW-XC give 4, 10, and 10 irreducible points for CO$_2$, NH$_3$, and urea. The matched fine-grid CO$_2$ and NH$_3$ AE basis controls use $10^{-4}$ and four irreducible points in both bases, while urea retains ten. The GTH-direct and GTH-1C calculations use $10^{-4}$ and give 4, 4, and 6. These finite tolerances recognize near-symmetries in the decimal coordinates without moving the atoms. Their numerical sensitivity is distinct from a full-versus-reduced-grid comparison.

\subsection{Paired energies and controls}
\label{sec:crystal-controls}

Table~\ref{tab:x23_total_si} gives all paired total energies. We use $E_{\rm latt}=E_{\rm solid}/Z-E_{\rm molecule}$ and $1~E_h=2625.4996394799$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$. The electronic diffusion Monte Carlo (DMC) references have statistical uncertainties of 0.2, 0.1, and 0.3~kJ~mol$^{-1}$ for CO$_2$, NH$_3$, and urea. Their estimated overall uncertainty is about 2~kJ~mol$^{-1}$, rather than the smaller statistical bars alone.\cite{DellaPia2024X23} Thermal and zero-point corrections to experimental sublimation enthalpies are not added to the DMC energies.

Device comparisons distinguish central processing unit (CPU) inference from accelerator inference through CUDA. The CPU/CUDA labels refer only to this device choice.

For NH$_3$, CO$_2$, and urea, respectively, the reported absolute AE errors are 0.015, 2.667, and 1.927~kJ~mol$^{-1}$, or 0.04\%, 9.1\%, and 1.8\% of the DMC binding magnitudes. GAPW-XC/GTH gives 1.874, 8.008, and 5.225~kJ~mol$^{-1}$, or 4.9\%, 27.2\%, and 4.8\%. Thus NH$_3$ has the smallest absolute error and CO$_2$ the largest absolute and fractional error in each reported representation. These errors describe the combined Skala--D3(BJ) protocol at the stated basis and geometry, not separate XC, dispersion, or deformation-energy components.

Refining the paired CO$_2$ quadrature from 150/770 to 200/974 changes $E_{\rm solid}/Z$ by $-0.110687$ and $E_{\rm molecule}$ by $-0.155241$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$. Their difference, $+0.044554$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$, measures the lattice-energy sensitivity. The $5^3$ crystal check uses 11 of 125 irreducible points, compared with four of 27 at $3^3$. For k-point, symmetry-tolerance, and device checks the molecule is unchanged, so the crystal shift per molecule is also the lattice-energy shift.

The accepted 1200~Ry cutoff result concerns only the CO$_2$ crystal and is not a paired binding-energy convergence test. The separate gas-phase calculation lacks a complete runtime-provenance record and is not used. CO$_2$ CPU/CUDA and urea-AE CPU/CUDA controls retain the respective executable and scientific input apart from model device selection. Their sub-0.001~kJ~mol$^{-1}$ changes constrain device effects for these cases. No complete-basis limit, basis-set superposition error (BSSE) correction, equilibrium volume, elastic response, or vibrational thermodynamics is inferred from the fixed-geometry energies.

\subsection{Paired all-electron basis sensitivity}
\label{sec:urea-basis}
\label{sec:crystal-basis}

The three basis tests use a matched crystal and gas molecule at each level. Within each pair of basis levels, the CPU model, geometry, AE Hamiltonian, one-centre reconstruction, dispersion parameters, 800/60~Ry cutoffs, 200/974 quadrature, k-point reduction, and SCF threshold are unchanged. The Urea series additionally separates the earlier quadrature refinement from basis enlargement. No geometry relaxation or reference-energy adjustment is involved.

\input{x23-basis-si}

At the triple-zeta basis, changing the paired quadrature from 150/770 to 200/974 shifts the lattice energy from $-124.637557$ to $-124.842875$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$, a change of $-0.205318$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$. Enlarging both phase bases to quadruple-zeta at 200/974 instead gives $-110.427467$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$, a change of $+14.415408$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$. The initial large AE binding energy is therefore substantially basis sensitive rather than a consequence of incomplete SCF convergence. The quadruple-zeta molecule and crystal converge in 114 and 29 steps, respectively, with final residuals $4.6\times10^{-7}$ and $3.4\times10^{-7}$. The reported larger-basis value differs from DMC by $-1.927467$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$ and from the three GTH results by approximately $+3.3$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$.

The same basis enlargement changes CO$_2$ from $-36.376366$ to $-32.066940$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$ and NH$_3$ from $-38.743444$ to $-38.184551$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$. The corresponding shifts are $+4.309426$ and $+0.558893$~kJ~mol$^{-1}$. CO$_2$ has a substantial basis contribution, while ammonia changes less and approaches the DMC value within its statistical uncertainty. The QZVPP crystal/molecule executions converge in 47/88 steps for CO$_2$ and 60/35 for NH$_3$. All final residuals are at or below $5\times10^{-7}$. The eight additional executions complete the same basis comparison for all three systems, rather than selecting a larger basis only where it improves a reference error.

An AE basis must resolve tightly localized core orbitals as well as the valence region, whereas the GTH Hamiltonian replaces the core electrons. Equal nominal zeta labels consequently do not establish equal completeness across the two core treatments. For a lattice energy, however, the decisive quantity is the differential basis error between crystal and molecule. The present basis enlargement changes that balance and can also alter BSSE. It does not isolate a core-only contribution or establish a complete-basis or counterpoise-corrected limit. Basis selection follows the paired numerical sensitivity, not removal of a system or adjustment of the functional to the DMC value.

\subsection{Other density-functional approximations}
\label{sec:crystal-literature}

Table~\ref{tab:x23-dft-context} compares the native results with the DMC references and published electronic lattice energies collected by Della Pia \emph{et al.}\cite{DellaPia2024X23,DellaPia2025MLIP} Their plane-wave calculations use hard projector augmented-wave (PAW) datasets, a 1000~eV cutoff, and dense system-specific k-point meshes. The PBE0+MBD values use FHI-aims. These are literature protocols, not same-basis native-code controls or Skala reruns. The MAE is the unweighted mean of $|E_{\rm latt}-E_{\rm latt}^{\rm DMC}|$ over CO$_2$, NH$_3$, and urea, using unrounded native energies and the reported literature values.

The native pseudopotential MAEs of 5.03--5.06~kJ~mol$^{-1}$ are close to revPBE+D3 (5.18), although several other dispersion-inclusive approximations have smaller three-crystal MAEs. The AE MAE is 1.54~kJ~mol$^{-1}$ with all three QZVPP pairs. NH$_3$ is accurate in both native core treatments, the QZVPP AE urea residual is 1.93~kJ~mol$^{-1}$, and pseudopotential urea errors are close to SCAN+rVV10 and vdW-DF2. CO$_2$ retains the largest AE residual, reduced to 2.67~kJ~mol$^{-1}$ by basis enlargement. The approximately 2~kJ~mol$^{-1}$ overall DMC uncertainty and the differing computational protocols caution against ranking methods by small residual differences.\cite{DellaPia2024X23} The results support promising, material-dependent condensed-phase transfer and substantial basis sensitivity, rather than universal strong overbinding.
